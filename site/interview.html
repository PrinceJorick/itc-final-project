    Shape an answer with STAR

    Whenever a question begins Tell me about a time…, answer with a short, true story in four moves:

    S: Situation
    Set the scene in one line: a class, an org, an OJT.
    T: Task
    What sat on your shoulders specifically?
    A: Action
    What you chose to do. Give this the most airtime.
    R: Result
    What changed afterwards? A number makes it stick.

    Keep the whole story inside two minutes, and keep three or four stories on hand that you can point at different questions.

    Four that almost always get asked

    OJT screens, BPO assessments, entry-level panels: the same handful opens them. Learn these cold.

    Tell me about yourself.

    Sixty seconds, three beats: what you study now, one achievement that matches this job, and why you applied for it.

    Why should we hire you?

    Take two or three strengths the post itself names, and pin one quick proof onto each.

    What's your expected salary?

    Look up the going range for the role in your city before the day, then offer a range and say you're open. For OJT, ask about the allowance instead.

    Can you work night shift?

    A BPO staple. Answer truthfully; if yes, mention you've planned around the commute and your sleep.

    Dress one notch up, carry a folder

    Aim slightly more formal than the office dresses on ordinary days. When unsure, it's perfectly fine to ask HR what the code is.

    Reliably safe choices

    • Corporate or office: collared shirt or blouse, slacks or a knee-length skirt, closed shoes; a blazer earns points.
    • BPO and tech: smart casual. Polo or blouse, dark pants, clean closed shoes.
    • Leave at home: ripped jeans, shorts, slippers or sandals, loud prints, heavy perfume.

    Inside your folder

    • Three to five printed copies of your resume
    • Two valid IDs, plus photocopies
    • A black ballpen for forms
    • 2×2 and 1×1 photos
    • Whatever papers the employer asked for
    • The interviewer's name and the office address

    When the interview happens online

    Plenty of first interviews in the Philippines now run on Zoom, Google Meet, or Teams. Give the call the same weight as an office visit.

    1. The day before

      Run a test call with a friend: app, camera, mic. Update the app itself, charge the laptop, and keep mobile data loaded in case the Wi-Fi drops.

    2. Your setup

      Face a window or a lamp so your face carries the light. A plain wall behind you, camera raised to eye level (stack books under it if needed).

    3. Ten minutes out

      Shut other apps, mute notifications, and let the household know you're in an interview. Keep your resume and notes open beside the call.

    4. Mid-call

      Look into the camera, not at your own preview, while speaking, and pause a beat before answering in case of lag. If the line drops, rejoin at once and apologize in one sentence.

    5. Afterwards

      Send a short thank-you within the day: one thing you talked about, and that your interest holds.
