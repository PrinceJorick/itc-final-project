    Answer with the STAR method

    For any question that starts with Tell me about a time…, tell a short story in four parts:

    S: Situation
    Where were you? (class, org, OJT)
    T: Task
    What did you need to do?
    A: Action
    What did you do? This is the longest part.
    R: Result
    What happened? Use a number if you can.

    Aim for one to two minutes. Prepare three or four stories you can reuse for different questions.

    Questions you'll almost surely hear

    These come up in Filipino interviews for OJT, BPO, and entry-level jobs. Prepare them first.

    Tell me about yourself.

    Present, past, future in one minute: what you study now, one achievement that fits the job, and why you're here.

    Why should we hire you?

    Match two or three strengths to what the job post asks for, each with a quick example.

    What is your expected salary?

    Research the range for the same role in your city on job sites before the interview. Give a range and say you're open to discussing it. For OJT, ask about allowance instead.

    Are you willing to work night shift?

    Common in BPO. Answer honestly. If yes, show you've thought about your commute and sleep.

    What to wear and bring

    Dress one step more formal than the job's daily wear. When in doubt, ask HR what the dress code is. That's a normal question.

    Safe choices

    • Corporate or office: collared shirt or blouse, slacks or a knee-length skirt, closed shoes. A blazer is a plus.
    • BPO and tech: smart casual: polo or blouse, dark pants, clean closed shoes.
    • Avoid: jeans with rips, shorts, slippers or sandals, loud prints, strong perfume.

    Bring in your brown envelope

    • 3–5 printed copies of your resume
    • Two valid IDs (and photocopies)
    • A black ballpen for forms
    • 2×2 and 1×1 ID photos
    • Any documents the employer asked for
    • The interviewer's name and the office address

    Online interview tips

    Many first interviews in the Philippines now happen over Zoom, Google Meet, or Teams. Treat the call like an office visit.

    1. The day before

      Test the app, camera, and mic with a friend. Update the app. Charge your laptop and keep mobile data ready as a backup in case of brownouts or Wi-Fi drops.

    2. Your setup

      Sit facing a window or lamp so your face is lit. Use a plain wall behind you. Put the camera at eye level, using books if you need to.

    3. Ten minutes before

      Close other apps and mute notifications. Tell your household you're in an interview. Keep your resume and notes open on the side.

    4. During the call

      Look at the camera when you speak, not at your own video. Pause a second before answering in case of lag. If the connection drops, rejoin right away and apologize briefly.

    5. After the call

      Send a short thank-you email within the day. Mention one thing you discussed and that you're still interested.
