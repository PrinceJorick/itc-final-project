    Answer with STAR

    For Tell me about a time… questions, tell a short story:

    Situation
    Where were you? A class, org, or OJT.
    Task
    What did you need to do?
    Action
    What did you do? Spend the most time here.
    Result
    What changed? Use a number if you can.

    Under two minutes. Prepare three stories you can reuse.

    Questions to plan for

    They come up in most OJT, BPO, and entry-level interviews.

    What's your expected salary?

    Check the range for the same role and city on job sites, then give a range and say you're open to the full package. For OJT, ask about the allowance.

    Can you work night shift?

    Common in BPO. Answer honestly. If yes, say you've planned your commute and sleep.

    Are you willing to relocate?

    Know your answer before you apply. If it depends, say on what: housing help, start date, or family.

    Why should we hire you?

    Match two or three strengths to the job post, each with a quick example.

    Online and technical interviews

    Online (Zoom, Meet, Teams)

    1. Day before: test camera and mic, charge up, keep mobile data as backup.

    2. Setup: face a light, plain background, camera at eye level.

    3. During: look at the camera. If the call drops, rejoin right away.

    Technical (IT, engineering, data)

    1. Know your projects: explain one in two minutes: the problem, your part, the tools, the result.

    2. Review the post's tools: one practice task each, like a SQL query or a spreadsheet formula.

    3. Think out loud: on a test, say your steps. "I'd check the cable first" beats silence.

    What to wear and bring

    Dress one step more formal than the office. Not sure? Ask HR.

    • Office: collared shirt or blouse, slacks or a knee-length skirt, closed shoes.
    • BPO and tech: smart casual, closed shoes.
    • Avoid: ripped jeans, shorts, slippers, strong perfume.

    Bring in a folder

    • 3–5 printed copies of your resume
    • Two valid IDs and photocopies
    • A black ballpen and ID photos

    After the interview

    Send a thank-you the same day. No reply after a week? Follow up once.

    Thank-you email

    Subject: Thank you for the interview, [Position]

Dear [Interviewer's name],

Thank you for meeting with me today about the [Position] role. I enjoyed learning about [one thing they said], and I'm even more interested in joining [Company].

[One line linking a project of yours to the job.]

Thank you again for your time. I look forward to hearing from you.

Sincerely,
[Your name]
[Mobile number]

    Follow-up after a week

    Subject: Following up on my application, [Position]

Dear [Interviewer's name],

I hope you're well. I'm following up on my interview on [date] for the [Position] role. I'm still very interested and would be glad to send anything else you need.

Thank you, and I hope to hear from you soon.

Sincerely,
[Your name]
[Mobile number]
