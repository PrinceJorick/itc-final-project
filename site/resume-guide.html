Writing a resume before you've ever been hired

You're carrying more material than you think. This guide walks through what to include, what local employers expect, and how to present school work so it reads as real preparation.

Roughly a 10-minute read.

Resume sections, one by one

For a student or fresh graduate, follow this order, and lift your strongest section toward the top.

Contact details
Complete name, mobile number, a professional address (firstname.lastname@…), city and province, plus a LinkedIn or portfolio link if you keep one.
Objective
Two lines naming the role you want and what you'd bring. Rewrite it for each application, and skip filler like "to gain experience in a challenging environment."
Education
School, degree, years attended, and honors such as Dean's List or a scholarship. While you're studying, this sits near the top.
Projects
Capstone, thesis, class projects, or things you built on your own. Name the tools and the outcome: this section is your experience section.
Organizations and leadership
Positions, committees, and events you helped run. Officers, rank-and-file members, and volunteers all count.
Work experience or OJT (optional)
Internships, part-time jobs, freelancing, the family business, tutoring. If you have none, leave the section out; that's normal at your stage.
Skills
Split into technical (Excel, Python, Canva, AutoCAD) and soft (teamwork, time management). Echo the exact wording the job post uses.
Certifications and awards
Online courses, seminar certificates, contest placings, and scholarships.

Philippine conventions

A lot of older Filipino templates are really a biodata: a form asking your height, religion, and parents' names. A modern resume is shorter and pointed.

Photo

Plenty of local employers still like a formal 2×2 photo, so attach one when the post or the office asks. Leave it off for online applications routed through applicant tracking systems (ATS) or for international companies, since photos can trip the parser — the builder here leaves it out for that reason.

Age, birthday, civil status, religion

None of these are required. Under the Anti-Age Discrimination in Employment Act (RA 10911), an employer cannot insist you declare your age or birth date during the application. Fill them in only when a form explicitly asks.

Address

City and province will do, as in Batangas City, Batangas. A resume that circulates doesn't need your house number.

Length

Hold it to one page. That's the working expectation for fresh graduates and OJT applicants. Print on short (Letter) or A4 bond.

Resume, CV, or biodata?

The three words get used interchangeably here. A resume is the one-to-two page summary a job asks for. A CV runs longer and serves academic or research applications. A biodata is a personal-details form. When a post says "CV," a fresh grad can almost always send a one-page resume.

PH tip: name the file properly

Send it as Lastname_Firstname_Resume.pdf. HR inboxes fill with files all named "resume.pdf."

Dos and don'ts

Do

  • Lead every bullet with an action verb: built, led, organized.
  • Attach numbers: team size, attendees, hours saved.
  • Borrow the exact skill words from the post.
  • Stick to a simple layout in standard fonts.
  • Proofread twice, then hand it to a friend.
  • Send it as PDF unless the office asks for Word.

Don't

  • Use an address like cutie_gamer99@….
  • List duties with no outcome ("in charge of…").
  • Tuck text inside tables, boxes, or images; ATS software may skip it.
  • Add salary figures or your reasons for leaving.
  • Include height, weight, or religion.
  • Stretch the truth. Expect the interviewer to pull on it.

Weak vs strong bullets

A strong bullet answers three things at once: What did you do? With what? And what changed because of it? Use the formula:

Action verb + what you did + tool used + result

  • WeakDid a website for our capstone.

    StrongBuilt a resort booking site with HTML, CSS, and PHP, replacing the guesthouse's paper logbook with online reservations.

  • WeakPart of the events committee.

    StrongRan registration for a 200-person career fair through Google Forms, cutting check-in to under a minute per guest.

  • WeakHandled the org page.

    StrongGrew the org's Facebook following from 600 to 1,400 in one semester with weekly Canva posts and event recaps.

  • WeakJoined a hackathon once.

    StrongPlaced 2nd of 30 teams in a regional hackathon after building a flood-alert prototype on Arduino with SMS alerts inside 24 hours.

No experience yet? Spend what you have.

Employers hiring OJT students and fresh grads aren't screening for a job history — they're checking that you can learn, work with people, and finish what you start. Here's where the evidence lives:

  • Capstones, theses, and class projects. Treat each like a job: your role, the tools, the outcome.
  • Org work. Officer or member, you planned events, handled money, drew posters, or recruited people.
  • Competitions. Hackathons, quiz bees, case competitions, sports, and debate all show skill and drive, winning optional.
  • Volunteering and NSTP. Community projects demonstrate initiative and teamwork.
  • Side work. Online selling, tutoring, freelance design, or shifts at the family business all belong.
  • Online courses. Free certificates from reputable platforms show you learn on your own time.
