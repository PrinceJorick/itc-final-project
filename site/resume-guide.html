Write a resume when you've never had a job

You have more to show than you think. This guide shows what to include, what Filipino employers expect, and how to make school work sound like real experience.

Resume sections, explained

For a student or fresh graduate, use this order. Put your strongest section near the top.

Contact details
Full name, mobile number, a professional email (firstname.lastname@…), city and province, and your LinkedIn or portfolio link if you have one.
Objective
Two lines: what role you want and what you can bring. Change it for every application. Skip clichés like "to gain experience in a challenging environment."
Education
School, degree, years attended, and honors such as Dean's List or scholarships. As a student, this goes near the top.
Projects
Capstone, thesis, class projects, or personal projects. Name the tools you used and the result. This is your "experience" section.
Organizations and leadership
Org positions, committees, and events you helped run. Officers, members, and volunteers all count.
Work experience or OJT (optional)
Internships, part-time jobs, freelance work, family business, or tutoring. Leave it out if you have none. That's normal.
Skills
Split into technical (Excel, Python, Canva, AutoCAD) and soft (teamwork, time management). Use the same words the job post uses.
Certifications and awards
Online courses, seminars with certificates, contest placements, and scholarships.

Philippine conventions

Many old Filipino templates are really a "biodata": a form with your height, religion, and parents' names. A modern resume is shorter and more focused.

Photo

Many local employers still like a formal 2×2 photo, so add one when the job post or employer asks for it. Leave it out when applying online to companies that use applicant tracking systems (ATS) or to international companies, because photos can break ATS parsing. The builder on this site leaves the photo out for that reason.

Age, birthday, civil status, religion

You don't need to include these. Under the Anti-Age Discrimination in Employment Act (RA 10911), employers may not require you to declare your age or birth date during the application process. Include them only if a form specifically asks.

Address

City and province are enough, for example Batangas City, Batangas. Your full house address isn't needed on a resume you send around.

Length

Keep it to one page. That is the usual expectation for fresh graduates and OJT applicants. Use short (Letter) or A4 bond paper when printing.

Resume, CV, or biodata?

In the Philippines, these words are often used as if they mean the same thing. A resume is a one-to-two page summary for a job. A CV is longer and is used for academic or research work. A biodata is a personal-details form. When a post asks for a "CV," a fresh grad can usually send a one-page resume.

PH tip: name your file properly

Save your resume as Lastname_Firstname_Resume.pdf. HR staff download hundreds of files named "resume.pdf."

Dos and don'ts

Do

  • Start each bullet with an action verb: built, led, organized.
  • Add numbers: team size, attendees, time saved.
  • Copy the exact skill words from the job post.
  • Use a simple layout with standard fonts.
  • Proofread twice, then ask a friend to read it.
  • Save and send as PDF unless the employer asks for Word.

Don't

  • Use an unprofessional email like cutie_gamer99@….
  • List duties without results ("in charge of…").
  • Put text in tables, text boxes, or images; ATS software may skip it.
  • Include salary expectations or reasons you left.
  • Add your height, weight, or religion.
  • Lie or exaggerate. Interviewers will ask about it.

Weak vs strong bullets

A strong bullet answers three questions: What did you do? How? What happened because of it? Use this formula:

Action verb + what you did + tool used + result

  • WeakMade a website for our capstone.

    StrongDeveloped a booking website for a local resort using HTML, CSS, and PHP, resulting in online reservations replacing paper logbooks.

  • WeakMember of the events committee.

    StrongOrganized registration for a 200-participant career fair using Google Forms, resulting in check-in times under one minute per guest.

  • WeakResponsible for the org's social media.

    StrongGrew the org's Facebook page from 600 to 1,400 followers in one semester by posting weekly Canva graphics and event recaps.

  • WeakJoined a hackathon.

    StrongPlaced 2nd of 30 teams in a regional hackathon by building a flood-alert prototype with Arduino and SMS notifications in 24 hours.

No experience yet? Use what you have.

Employers hiring OJT students and fresh grads don't expect a job history. They want proof that you can learn, work with people, and finish things. Here's where to find it:

  • Capstone, thesis, and class projects. Treat each one like a job: your role, the tools, the result.
  • Org work. Officer or member, you planned events, handled money, made posters, or recruited people.
  • Competitions. Hackathons, quiz bees, case competitions, sports, and debate show skill and drive, even if you didn't win.
  • Volunteering and NSTP. Community projects show initiative and teamwork.
  • Side work. Online selling, tutoring, freelance design, or helping in a family business all count.
  • Online courses. Free certificates from reputable platforms show you learn on your own.
