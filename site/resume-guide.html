Resume sections

Use this order as a student or fresh graduate.

Contact details
Full name, mobile number, a professional email, city and province, and LinkedIn or portfolio if you have one.
Objective
Two lines: the role you want and what you bring. Tailor it to each job.
Education
School, degree, years, and honors like Dean's List or scholarships.
Projects
Capstone, thesis, class, or personal projects, with the tools you used and the result. This is your experience.
Organizations and leadership
Roles, committees, and events. Officers, members, and volunteers all count.
Work experience or OJT (optional)
Internships, part-time jobs, freelancing, tutoring. Leave it out if you have none.
Skills
Technical (Excel, Python, Canva) and soft (teamwork, time management). Match the job post's wording.
Certifications and awards
Online courses, seminar certificates, contest placements, and scholarships.

Philippine conventions

Many old Filipino templates are really a biodata. A modern resume is shorter and focused on the job.

Photo
Add a formal 2×2 photo only when the employer asks. Leave it off for online and international applications, since photos can confuse applicant tracking systems (ATS).
Age, birthday, civil status, religion
Not needed. Under the Anti-Age Discrimination in Employment Act (RA 10911), employers can't require your age or birth date when you apply.
Address
City and province are enough, for example Batangas City, Batangas.
Length
One page. Print on short (Letter) or A4 bond paper.
Resume, CV, or biodata?
A resume is a one-page job summary, a CV is longer and academic, and a biodata is a personal-details form. If a post asks for a CV, a one-page resume is usually fine.

PH tip: name your file

Save it as Lastname_Firstname_Resume.pdf, not "resume.pdf."

Dos and don'ts

Do

  • Start bullets with action verbs: built, led, organized.
  • Add numbers: team size, attendees, time saved.
  • Use the job post's exact skill words.
  • Keep a simple layout and standard fonts.
  • Proofread, then ask a friend to check.

Don't

  • Use an email like cutie_gamer99@….
  • List duties without results ("in charge of…").
  • Put text in tables or images; ATS software may skip it.
  • Add your height, weight, or religion.
  • Exaggerate. Interviewers will ask about it.

Weak vs strong bullets

A strong bullet says what you did, how, and what changed.

Action verb + what you did + tool used + result

  • WeakMade a website for our capstone.

    StrongBuilt a resort booking site with HTML, CSS, and PHP, replacing a paper logbook with online reservations.

  • WeakMember of the events committee.

    StrongRan registration for a 200-person career fair with Google Forms, cutting check-in to under a minute per guest.

  • WeakJoined a hackathon.

    StrongPlaced 2nd of 30 teams in a regional hackathon with an Arduino flood-alert prototype built in 24 hours.

No experience yet? Use what you have.

Employers hiring students want proof that you can learn, work with others, and finish things. Look here:

  • Projects: capstone, thesis, and class work. Name your role, tools, and result.
  • Org work: events you planned, budgets you handled, people you recruited.
  • Competitions: hackathons, quiz bees, debate, and sports, even without a win.
  • Volunteering and NSTP: community work shows initiative.
  • Side work and courses: tutoring, online selling, freelancing, and free certificates.
